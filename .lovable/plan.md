# Make every page Google already shows worth clicking

Google is showing these pages again, now they need to earn the click. Two parts: give the 557 bare state pages real state-specific substance, then rewrite the titles and descriptions on every page Google shows, starting with the pages already near the top.

## Part A — Fill out the 557 bare state pages

Right now these pages are mostly the general topic article with the state's name swapped in. Google has noticed them, but there's nothing on them a reader couldn't find on the page for the next state.

| Topic | Pages | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Criminal law | 131 | domestic violence charges, bail, expungement |
| Insurance law | 120 | denied life insurance claims, bad faith, subrogation |
| Employment law | 116 | severance, wrongful termination, final paycheck |
| Landlord and tenant | 97 | security deposits, eviction notice periods |
| Personal injury | 69 | contingency fees, deadlines to sue, damage caps |
| Auto accident | 38 | fault rules, minimum insurance |
| AI and tech law | 22 | right to repair, deepfakes, social media |

Each page gets a state-specific block (the same block the five rebuilt pages already use) containing:
- **Quick answer:** two or three sentences answering the exact search for that state.
- **The state's actual rule:** the law that applies (by name and section number), the deadline, the dollar limits, and the agency or court that handles it.
- **One small original table**, such as penalty ranges, deadlines, notice periods or caps.
- **Three to five state-specific questions** taken from what people actually searched, with answers.
- **Where to file or complain**, using the court and agency data we already hold.
- **Links:** the matching calculator or form, neighbouring states, the topic hub and the city lawyer pages for that state.
- A "checked on" date, the author byline and the legal disclaimer.

**Accuracy rule:** every figure must come from the official state source or the data we have already checked. If a figure can't be confirmed, it is left out rather than guessed.

**Order:**
1. Pages Google shows most often first (about 150 pages that had 3 or more showings).
2. Then the remaining pages, one topic at a time: criminal, insurance, employment, landlord, personal injury, auto, AI and tech.
3. Shared state facts are researched once per state and topic, then reused across that topic's pages, so each state's facts stay consistent.

**What this needs from you:** writing and checking 557 pages uses AI credits. That's roughly the same credit cost as the blog translations, so the credit top-up comes first.

## Part B — Rewrite titles and descriptions

### B1. Pages already near the top of Google (do first, no AI needed)
| Page | Position in Google now |

| About | 5 |

Each gets a title that leads with what the searcher typed, then a concrete benefit or number, under 60 characters. Each description gives a direct answer and a reason to click, under 155 characters. The About and Contact pages say clearly what the site is ("free legal calculators and plain-English guides"), so people searching the brand name click through.

### B2. The page templates (covers thousands of pages at once)
Update the title and description patterns for state guides, glossary terms, contract clauses, lawyer city pages and tools, so every page gets the state, the outcome and the number in its title, not a generic "Guide | LegallySpoken".

### B3. Check the results
Four weeks after publishing, compare clicks and click rate per page in Search Console. Titles that don't improve get a second version.

## Part C — Keep it working
- Refresh the list of pages Google shows every month, so new winners stay visible.
- Leave pages that have never been shown hidden until they get real substance.
- Resubmit the page list to Google after each publish.

## Order of work
1. B1: the pages near the top. Fast, and no AI credits needed.
2. B2: the title patterns.
3. Publish and resubmit the page list to Google.
4. Part A, in batches of about 50 pages, publishing after each batch (after the credit top-up).
5. Blog translations, then the rest of the earlier plan (Spain pages, new calculators, the citable data page).

## Technical notes
- State depth data goes into `src/data/nearMissDepth.ts` (split per pillar into `src/data/stateDepth/<pillar>.ts`), rendered by `NearMissDepthBlock`, and counted by the existing depth gate.
- Generated by a script that uses `_aiAstra.mjs` with strict JSON output and a sources field. Records without a source are rejected.
- Title templates live in `StateClusterArticlePage`, `LegalTermPage`, `LegalClausePage`, `LocalLawyersCityPage` and `ToolPageLayout`. The one-off titles go in the Head props for each page. The Colorado article title is updated in `blog_posts`.
- FAQ JSON-LD and `dateModified` are refreshed per page. The `provenDemandUrls.ts` refresh comes from a Search Console page query.
